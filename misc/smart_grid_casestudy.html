Foundation

What is a smart grid?

The traditional grid works in one direction — power plants generate, wires carry, homes consume. Nobody knows real-time usage until the meter reader visits once a month.

A smart grid adds two-way communication. Sensors, smart meters, and control software let the grid see itself in real time — automatically absorbing rooftop solar, shifting loads during stress, and dispatching batteries in milliseconds.

Key insight: Grids built for large coal and gas plants can't handle millions of small, variable solar and wind sources. Smart grid technology is what makes the clean energy transition physically possible.

Four core components

AMI — Advanced Metering Infrastructure

Smart meters sending 15-min usage data. Foundation for everything else.

DMS / SCADA

Real-time network visibility. Outages detected in 3 min instead of 45 min.

Demand Response

Smart thermostats and loads turned down during grid stress. Cheaper than a peaker plant.

BESS / Virtual Power Plants

Grid-scale or aggregated home batteries. Respond faster than any gas turbine.

Case Study 03 — India

India's Smart Grid Mission & TPDDL Delhi

RDSS — Rs 3,03,758 Cr scheme, 2021

India's grid problem is AT&C losses — electricity generated but either lost to a leaking wire or stolen and never billed. National average in 2020–21: 22.2%. That's 1 in 5 units never becoming revenue.

RDSS (~USD 36.5B) targets 250 million smart prepaid meters and feeder separation to fix this.

How they did it

  • 250,000+ smart meters deployed from 2007–08; full SCADA on 11kV network
  • Online transformer monitoring — 30–40% fewer failures
  • Anti-theft detection baked into smart metering — biggest single driver

Technology was the enabler — not the sole cause. Operational culture and billing enforcement mattered equally.

The backlash — 2010

Installations hit San Joaquin Valley in hot summer 2009. Bills spiked. Customers blamed the meters. Bakersfield voted to ban installations.

CPUC found: Meters were accurate. Bills rose from a heat wave + tiered rates + a rate increase. PG&E's real failure: they told customers nothing about the swap or how tiered pricing worked.

What actually worked
  • Outage detection: 45 min → 3–5 min via automated meter signals
  • Theft recovery: ~$96 million recovered in first few years
  • Operational savings: $47–60 million/year — no more manual reads
  • Peak demand: SmartAC program cut ~600 MW on hot summer afternoons
What it triggered
  • First US state smart meter privacy rules — CPUC July 2011
  • First formal opt-out program — $75 upfront + $10/month
  • Programme was roughly NPV break-even — $1.0–1.5B benefits vs $2.2B cost

Hardware worked fine. Communication strategy failed — and set back smart meter adoption nationally for years.

Conclusion

Three things four case studies prove

01

Grid-scale storage is proven

Hornsdale saved AUD 150M in 2.5 years and responded in 140 ms. The business case is in audited financial statements — not projections.

02

Technology is the easy part

Xcel's hardware worked. PG&E's meters were accurate. Both failed on the human side — communication, change management, and realistic planning.

03

Loss reduction = India's killer app

TPDDL proved smart metering pays for itself through recovered revenue in high-theft markets. India's RDSS has the right idea — 250M meters at scale is the challenge.

What's next

  • Vehicle-to-Grid — every EV as a dispatchable battery
  • AI forecasting — predict solar/wind 4–6 hrs ahead
  • 5G replacing aging SCADA radio links at substations

Unsolved problems

  • Cybersecurity — Ukraine grid attacks (2015, 2016) showed the risk
  • Privacy — 15-min interval data reveals when you're home or away
  • Interoperability — incompatible protocols strand installed hardware
